## Decision policy

No architectural decision is accepted merely because it appears in a diagram or candidate table. **Accepted** is recorded as `Current` with a linked ADR and evidence. This repository currently has no accepted implementation-technology ADRs.

Create an ADR when a choice is consequential, difficult to reverse, contested, security/privacy relevant, or required by a delivery gate. An ADR records: title/status; context; decision; considered options; consequences; accountable owner; decision date; evidence; validation/rollback; affected documents; and superseded ADRs. Review dates are not invented; review triggers are explicit.

Store the first accepted ADR under `docs/adr/NNNN-short-title.md` and add it to this register. Do not create the directory or an ADR solely to make the structure appear complete. Proposed and Open items remain here until evidence supports a decision.

## Publication-substrate decision

### Decision and default

Publication is a replaceable corroboration service, not part of proof semantics. The proposed product default is a **local signed receipt** returned with an exportable proof package. Customer-managed persistence is the preferred durability extension when a customer needs custody, retention control, or internal audit integration. An append-only transparency log is a candidate when mutually distrustful parties need externally auditable ordering or omission detection. Midnight is a candidate only when a documented relying-party workflow specifically needs the governance or shared-state properties of that network and the evidence gate below is passed.

No live ledger, including Midnight, may become a supported product workflow merely because it offers timestamping, immutability, marketing value, or technical feasibility. Before implementation or product support, the product owner MUST record a named relying party, the decision the publication record changes, why signed receipts and customer-managed storage are insufficient, required witnesses/readers, acceptable disclosure, volume and retention, latency/finality/availability targets, revocation expectations, independent-verification method, and willingness to bear integration and operating cost. The accountable reviewers in the register MUST approve that record and an ADR backed by a representative workflow demonstration. In the absence of that evidence, live-ledger work remains an unsupported experiment and the deterministic mock remains the only required CI publication dependency.

The substrate does not repair an invalid proof, establish telemetry truth, or become the authoritative verifier. Proof verification MUST remain possible from the envelope and authenticated verification artifacts without contacting a publication service. A relying-party policy may separately require a valid publication receipt; failure of that requirement changes receipt status and policy acceptance as specified by the envelope contract, not the underlying cryptographic proof result.

### Evidence comparison

The ratings below are decision hypotheses to validate with contract tests, measurements, threat/privacy review, and relying-party discovery; they are not vendor claims. “Strong” means the property is inherent or directly controllable in the described deployment, while “conditional” means it depends on governance, replication, anchoring, or operations.

| Requirement | Local signed receipt | Append-only transparency log | Customer-managed persistence | Midnight publication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Multi-party trust | Weak for shared ordering: parties trust the signer and can present inconsistent histories unless receipts are cross-witnessed | Strong when independently operated monitors verify consistency and gossip checkpoints; otherwise the log operator can omit or fork views | Conditional: strong customer custody, but external parties still trust customer controls, signatures, and exports | Conditional shared consensus reduces reliance on one publisher, but introduces network, contract, governance, and client trust |
| Privacy leakage | Lowest: disclose point-to-point and keep the package offline | Medium: public or broadly visible digests, timing, frequency, and correlation remain observable even when payloads are hidden | Low and customer-controlled, subject to customer access, backup, and export policy | Highest default exposure: globally replicated durable metadata, transaction sender/linkage, timing, frequency, and contract interactions require an explicit allowlist |
| Cost | Lowest implementation and operation cost; signature and package handling only | Moderate service, monitor, witness, and storage cost | Customer bears database, backup, access-control, and migration cost | Variable fees plus SDK, contract, wallet/key, monitoring, reconciliation, and network operations; benchmark total cost at expected and burst volume |
| Latency | Immediate after local signing | Usually low, but inclusion and witnessed checkpoints add delay | Usually low on the customer network | Network submission plus confirmation/finality delay; measure percentiles and backlog behavior rather than assuming them |
| Finality | Signature is final as an artifact but proves no global order; key compromise affects trust | Cryptographic consistency gives append-only history, while usable finality depends on checkpoint/witness policy | Defined by the customer's commit, replication, and backup policy; no independent global finality | Defined by the pinned network/profile and may include probabilistic or protocol finality and reorganization handling |
| Availability | Works offline and fails only with the local signer/package path; loss before export loses the record | Depends on log and monitor diversity; cached proofs/checkpoints can be checked offline | Controlled by customer topology and recovery practice; can operate without vendor connectivity | Depends on network, client, RPC/provider, contract, wallet/key, and fee availability; verification must continue during outage |
| Revocation/correction | Cannot erase issued receipts; publish signed status/revocation material and preserve the original | Append-only correction or revocation entries preserve history; monitors must apply freshness rules | Customer can correct/delete under policy, with signed audit history needed to expose changes externally | On-ledger data is not erased; contract/status updates can supersede it but readers need explicit cutoff and trust rules |
| Retention/deletion | Holder-controlled copies support minimization but prevent guaranteed deletion after distribution | Log replication intentionally resists deletion; retention must be bounded before publication | Best fit for contractual retention, legal hold, export, and deletion under customer control | Replication and ledger permanence make deletion guarantees impractical; publish no data whose lifecycle requires erasure |
| Independent verification | Strong for signature and proof verification with pinned signer keys and exported status artifacts; no global completeness guarantee | Strong for inclusion and consistency with portable proofs, signed checkpoints, and independent monitors | Conditional on exportable signed records, authenticated snapshots, and non-proprietary formats | Conditional on a portable receipt/state proof, pinned ledger profile and trust anchors, and an independent client route; a vendor API response is insufficient |

Evidence must test the important failure modes rather than selecting by aggregate score. In particular: demonstrate equivocation detection for a transparency log; backup/restore and customer-only operation for customer persistence; offline receipt verification plus signer rotation/revocation for local receipts; and fees, metadata correlation, timeout, retry, duplicate submission, reorganization/finality, network/provider outage, contract upgrade, key recovery, and independently reproduced receipt verification for Midnight. Record measured workload assumptions and the exact network/software versions with the results.

### Common publication adapter contract

Every substrate MUST implement the same versioned publication port and pass one substrate-neutral conformance suite. The request contains only an idempotency key, the digest of the canonical envelope with its receipt omitted, an approved metadata map, and an explicit publication policy/profile identifier. The result contains a substrate/profile identifier, stable record identifier, recorded time and its authority, payload digest, lifecycle state (`pending`, `recorded`, `final`, `superseded`, or `failed`), finality evidence, and a portable receipt or error. Implementations MUST support idempotent retry, status lookup, bounded timeout/cancellation, deterministic error classification, reconciliation, and verification of receipts without exposing SDK-, database-, log-, or ledger-native types to the domain or verifier.

The canonical claim, public-input ordering, proof bytes, policy/key selection, cryptographic evaluation, and non-publication result dimensions MUST be identical for identical authenticated inputs regardless of whether publication is disabled or which adapter is selected. Substrate-specific receipt verification and finality remain in the typed publication dimension; only an authenticated policy requirement may affect policy acceptance. Switching substrates therefore requires a publication-profile/version and migration decision, but MUST NOT require a new proof statement or allow an adapter to reinterpret proof validity. Conformance fixtures MUST run against the no-op/deterministic mock and every supported implementation; a live service is not required to verify a proof package.

## Current invariants versus decisions

The `Current` claims concern repository reality, documentation governance, and the envelope version 1 validity-window semantics in ADR-0001: the repository is documentation-only, this register is the decision process, and no implementation-technology ADR exists. MVP scope and architecture are Proposed constraints to guide the first implementation; they become accepted decisions only through accountable review. Open items block their stated gates. Deferred items are not implied future commitments.
